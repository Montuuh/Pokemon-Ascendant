# Playtest — v0.3 Identity through Evolution

**Date:** 2026-09-19 · **Build:** v0.3 code complete · **Tester:** internal (Claude), 120-seed harness +
the Playwright route + hand-driven passes on the three new screens.

> Internal report. The v0.3 exit criteria that matter — "testers describe the evolution choice as the moment
> their run became theirs" and "a tester can say what they bought at the Dojo and why" — need humans. This
> covers what a machine can answer: does it work, does it hold together, and what did measuring it teach us.

---

## 1. Does the choice change the run?

Yes, and more than expected. The clearest evidence is accidental: the whole-run harness has to pick an
archetype like a player would, and **how it picks moved the Charmander line's win rate by 7 percentage points
on its own** — 20 % when it scored the payload's raw power gain, 27 % when it scored the resulting four-card
kit. Nothing about the game changed between those two numbers. Only the archetype did.

The reason is Pillar 2's: a Vanguard Charmeleon's pool is almost all Melee, and a Melee-only kit is a dead hand
every turn that Pokémon is benched. Picking by power alone dropped Ember, the one card it could play from the
bench. That is the branch system working — the archetypes are genuinely different, not three coats of paint —
and it is also the defect described in §4 below.

## 2. The three screens

**Evolution (§3.6).** Reads well. The morph holds the "before" for 900 ms, bleaches out and lands on the
"after"; the three cards sit beside it the whole time so the celebration never hides the decision. Each card
states its payload as a diff — struck-through old name, arrow, new name with its type glyph, power and AP —
and marks the upgrades that will land in your active 4, which is the thing you actually need to know. Confirm
is disabled until you pick. Reduced motion cuts straight to the after with no morph and no flash.

**Move Manager (§6.7.2).** One click moves a card between the pool and the four slots. At the cap an
unequipped card is locked rather than silently swapping something out, which is the right way round: the
budget is the point. *Auto* applies the kit rules. The Box row carries a badge with the number of moves
waiting, so the pool is discoverable without going looking.

**The Dojo (§2.9.4).** Warm front-end chrome like the Centre, not the dark stage theme. Box on the left,
services in the middle, the live deck on the right — so what a purchase does to the active 4 is on screen
while you are deciding whether to make it. The one-service credit reads clearly: buy something and every other
row greys out at once.

## 3. Balance, measured

120 seeds per starter, taking the Dojo when the route offers it:

| Starter | Win | Depth (of 8) | Turns | Top level | Evolutions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bulbasaur | 64 % | 6.3 | 29 | 15.1 | 3.0 |
| Charmander | 28 % | 6.3 | 26 | 13.4 | 2.2 |
| Squirtle | 85 % | 7.4 | 29 | 17.3 | 3.6 |

Skipping the Dojo costs 2 pp on Bulbasaur and 3 pp on Squirtle, which is the answer to "is a sculpting stop
worth a fight?" — just about, and only because the route grew a layer to pay for it (see §4).

**The Charmander spread is the open question.** 28 % against 85 % is flavour-correct — Rock/Ground resists
Fire and folds to Grass and Water — and it is not a bug, but it is not a balance anyone would ship either. The
designed fix is v0.5's Gym fork: with two Gyms of four to choose between, a Fire start picks one it can beat.
Tuning Region 1 around a single mandatory Rock Gym would move the problem to whichever starter loses the new
matchup. Flagged, not fixed.

## 4. What the harness caught

Three defects, none of which a single-fight test could have found.

**The route could not afford a service node.** You clear exactly one node per layer, so the Dojo did not sit
*beside* a fight — it replaced one, taking the route from six fights to five against an XP table tuned for
six. Charmander fell from 28 % to 11 %. The route grew from seven layers to eight and the arc came back. This
is worth remembering for every future service node: on a one-node-per-layer map, adding a stop costs a fight.

**The auto-pick could build an all-Melee kit.** §6.3.6.5 forbids it and nothing enforced it, because until
v0.3 the pool *was* the learnset and the learnsets are authored to comply. Branch payloads broke that
assumption. `autoPickMoves` now buys a Ranged card with its least useful slot, and Squirtle went from 89 % to
100 % on the 18-seed sample the moment it did.

**`STARTER_IDS.map(simulate)` passed the array index as the policy.** Every starter but the first ran with a
nonsense policy object and the pacing numbers collapsed to a third of their real value. Caught because the
number was absurd, not because a test failed — a reminder that a harness needs its own sanity checks.

## 5. Defects found and fixed outside the harness

- **Stale-`run` closure.** `visitDojo` took the run state by value while its `step` callback replaced it, so
  the post-purchase re-pick read a state one action old. Now it takes a getter.
- **`set-moves` was rejected in `swap-or-skip` and `ended`.** The phase list was an allow-list of four; it is
  now stated as what it is *not* — locked inside a fight, locked after the run ends, free everywhere else.
- **The Dojo rendered in the dark stage theme** over a warm light gradient. Node service screens are
  front-end chrome.
- **An id collision I caused and then undid.** I authored 24 branch moves before reading
  `catalogs/species-r1.md`, which already specifies every line's branch table. One of my ids, `seed-bomb`, was
  already Bulbasaur's Mastery Lv1. The invented set was removed and the catalogue's 27 moves ported in its
  place. The catalogue is the content-authoring source; I should have opened it first.
